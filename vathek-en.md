This is not, of course, to suggest that a contemporary startup reenacts Vathek. It is rather to recognize a recurrence: when promise becomes limitless, desire tends to lose its measure. Excess takes on a different form today—billion-dollar funding rounds, the language of infinite scale, the presupposition that there is always more: more data, more capacity, more reach. The underlying logic is the same: the next step is not merely progress, but تجاوز—transgression, surpassing.

There is also a second layer to this recurrence, one of a geopolitical and imaginative nature. Beckford wrote of an invented Orient, filtered through European fantasy and the Orientalist taste of his time. In speaking today of emerging technological hubs—in India, Southeast Asia, the Gulf—we risk repeating the gesture: transforming real places into symbolic stages of promise, projecting onto them the desires and expectations of a center that still imagines itself as the center.

Between the caliph descending into hell in search of knowledge and the investor betting on the next funding round, the language has changed entirely. The impulse, however—that of always wanting beyond the limit—may well be the same.
